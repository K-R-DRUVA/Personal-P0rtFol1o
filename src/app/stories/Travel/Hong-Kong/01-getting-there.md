# Getting There Was Harder Than Getting In


> **Location:** Hong Kong 🇭🇰
>
> **Date:** January 10, 2026
>
> **Part 1 of 3**
If you're reading this years from now, you'll probably remember Hong Kong.

You probably won't remember everything it took just to get there.

When I first got the opportunity to attend the conference, the biggest challenge wasn't the research paper.

It wasn't the visa.

It wasn't the flight.

It was convincing my parents to let me go.

Looking back, I completely understand why they were hesitant.

It was my first international trip.

I was still a college student.

And I was travelling to another country without my family.

It wasn't an easy "yes."

I still remember my dad coming to college to talk to one of my professors about the conference.

The funny part?

He accidentally walked into her cabin...

...while she was having lunch.

I can only imagine how awkward that must have been.

Somehow, despite all the awkwardness, the conversations, and the convincing...

they finally agreed.

Looking back now...

I'm incredibly grateful they did.

Because if they hadn't...

none of the stories you're about to read would have existed.

# Before the Flight

There are a lot of things I'll probably forget about this trip.

This conversation isn't one of them.

I still remember taking the airport bus to the airport with my dad.

I was scared and excited at the same point.

Not because I was flying.

Not because I was travelling abroad.

I was scared because it was my first international conference. My first time leaving India. My first time standing on a stage where I wasn't just another college student.

Dad noticed.

I still remember the drive to the airport.

I was nervous.

Not because of the flight.

Not because of Hong Kong.

I was nervous because this was my first time leaving India on my own.

Dad could tell.

For most of the drive, he spoke about random things.

Then, just before we reached the airport, he became serious.

He looked at me and said,

> "Nothing is going to happen if you mess up."

> "The world isn't going to fall apart."

Then came the part that still makes me smile.

> "And stop saying 'fuck' every five minutes."

I had a terrible habit back then.

Whenever I got excited, surprised, frustrated, or even happy...

"fuck" somehow became punctuation.

Then he looked at me again and said,

> "You're not representing your college anymore."

> "You're representing your country."

That sentence hit differently.

I don't remember exactly what I replied.

But I do remember looking out of the bus window and thinking,

Whatever happens over the next few days... I'm not going to let fear stop me from living this experience.

# Arrival

Landing in Hong Kong was probably the first time I truly realized that I was no longer in India.

Everything felt... compact.

The roads.

The buildings.

The traffic.

There were skyscrapers everywhere.

It almost felt like the city had no option but to grow upwards because there simply wasn't any land left.

As we drove through the city, I couldn't stop staring out of the window.

Every building looked like something straight out of a movie.

I remember thinking,

"This place is going to be very different."

I wasn't wrong.

# Chungking Mansions
![Chungking Mansion.](/images/stories/chungking-mansion-entrance.jpg)
To save some money, I had booked a room in one of Hong Kong's most iconic buildings Chungking Mansions.

If you've never heard of it before, neither had I.

Only after reaching Hong Kong did I realize the reputation the place had.

People online described it as everything from a backpacker's paradise to one of the sketchiest buildings in the city.

To make things even better...

I remember hearing that just a day before I checked in, a Hong Kong police officer had been murdered nearby.

Safe to say...

my confidence wasn't exactly increasing.

But after spending some time there, I slowly started understanding why so many people still stayed there.
![Chungking Mansion Overview.](/images/stories/chungking-mansion.jpg)
It wasn't fancy.

It wasn't luxurious.

It was affordable.

Walking through the building felt like walking through a miniature version of the world.

Every few steps I heard a different language.

Hindi.

Urdu.

Bengali.

English.

Cantonese.

People from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, different African countries, Vietnam...

everyone seemed to pass through Chungking Mansions at some point.

The corridors were crowded.

The elevators were tiny.

The rooms...

even tinier.

Surprisingly...

I never really felt uncomfortable.

Maybe four years in a boys' hostel had already prepared me for controlled chaos.

Compared to hostel life...

Chungking Mansions honestly wasn't that bad.

# Meeting Boom

Before the conference officially began, we had to collect our conference kits the previous day.

That was also the day I met Boom.

I still remember waiting at the bus stop in what felt like the middle of rural Hong Kong.

There weren't many people around.

Then I noticed this guy.

To me, he looked Chinese.

Later I'd find out he was actually from Thailand.

He looked at me...

smiled...

and I remember thinking,

Okay... at least one person here doesn't think I'm completely lost.

A Couple hours later, I found out what was going through his head.

He told me,

> "I saw an Indian guy standing at the bus stop, and I thought... he's definitely coming to the conference."

Meanwhile...

my brain was much simpler.

> "The only Chinese-looking guy who smiled at me."

That one smile made the place feel a little less unfamiliar.

# Breakfast

That's when we actually started talking.

We ended up having Breakfast at the SChool Canteen/Mess.

There was one small problem.

I'd never tasted authentic soy noodles before.

One bite...

My taste buds immediately started negotiating with my stomach.

I was trying my absolute best not to offend anyone.

Everyone noticed.

Everyone laughed.

I laughed.

And somehow...

over a breakfast that my taste buds completely disagreed with...


we became friends.

![Breakfast with the people who would make the rest of my Hong Kong trip unforgettable.](/images/stories/first_time_meeting_thai_friends.jpg)


*From right to left: Japan, Boom, me, Prof.Cat and Prof.Air  from Thailand—the five of us had no idea this breakfast was the beginning of a friendship that would define the rest of the trip.*

At that moment...

they were simply people I'd met over breakfast.

I had absolutely no idea they'd spend the next two days teaching me how to use chopsticks, laughing through my mistakes, wandering around Macau with me, and becoming some of my favourite memories from Hong Kong.

# Back at the Hotel

Later that evening, after returning from the conference with my new Thai friends, I squeezed into one of those tiny elevators.

When I say tiny...

I mean there was barely enough room for two people.

It was just me and a Chinese woman who looked like she was in her mid-twenties.

Completely by accident, I glanced at her phone.

She was texting someone about the hotel.

> "It's really cheap... the room is okay... but the Indian staff are kind of creepy."

I immediately looked away and tried my best not to laugh.

A few seconds later, she quietly put her phone back into her pocket, looked at me, and asked,

> "Excuse me... where's the exit?"

I pointed her in the right direction.

She thanked me.

I smiled.

We walked out of the building in opposite directions.

I'll probably never meet her again.

But for some reason...

that's one of those tiny moments from Hong Kong that has stayed with me.
